1.0 SYSTEM OBJECTIVE AND OPERATIONAL PURPOSE
Generative models in machine learning require two distinct mechanisms:
1. A sampling mechanism to draw base noise vectors.
2. A transport mechanism to map noise into the target empirical distribution.
Standard frameworks decouple these mechanisms. This technical order specifies an architecture that couples the base sampling density and the optimal transport map into a single scalar convex potential [05:51].
2.0 BASELINE SPECIFICATION: STANDARD MOMENT MEASURES
2.1 Theoretical Framework
Let \rho \in \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^d) be a target probability measure. Assume \rho admits a first moment, has barycenter at zero (\int x \, d\rho(x) = 0), and is not supported on any affine hyperplane [00:02].
The classical theorem of Cordero-Erausquin and Klartag (2015) establishes the existence of a convex potential U: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R} such that:
\rho = (\nabla U)_\# \mu_U
where \mu_U is the Gibbs (log-concave) prior density defined by:
d\mu_U(x) = \frac{1}{Z_U} \exp(-U(x)) \, dx
with normalizing partition function Z_U = \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} \exp(-U(x)) \, dx [00:18].
2.2 Optimal Transport Duality
By Brenier’s theorem, the gradient \nabla U of a convex potential is the unique L^2-optimal transport map pushing forward \mu_U to \rho under the quadratic Monge cost c(x, y) = \frac{1}{2}\Vert{}x - y\Vert{}^2 [04:13].
Santambrogio (2016) proved this identity by showing that \mu_U minimizes the functional:
\mathcal{J}(\mu) = \mathcal{H}(\mu) + \mathcal{W}_2^2\text{-dual}(\mu, \rho)
where \mathcal{H}(\mu) = \int \mu \log \mu represents the negative Shannon entropy and the second term is the dual Kantorovich formulation [03:03].
3.0 FAILURE MODE ANALYSIS: MOMENT MEASURE DEGENERACY
Standard moment measures fail in generative modeling applications [06:13].
3.1 Dual Representation via Legendre Conjugate
The minimization problem over convex functions can be rewritten using the Kullback-Leibler divergence [07:11]:
\min_U \left\{ \mathrm{KL}\left(\rho \,\Vert{}\, \mu_{U^*}\right) + \log Z_{U^*} + \log Z_U \right\}
where U^*(y) = \sup_x \{\langle x, y \rangle - U(x)\} is the Legendre-Fenchel conjugate of U.
This functional enforces proximity between the target \rho and \mu_{U^*}. It provides no guarantee that the base prior \mu_U aligns with \rho [08:04].
3.2 Covariance Inversion (Gaussian Pathology)
Let \rho = \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma) be a multivariate Gaussian target with covariance tensor \Sigma \succ 0 [08:34].
* The exact analytical solution yields quadratic potential U(x) = \frac{1}{2} x^\top \Sigma x + C.
* The base prior distribution is: \mu_U = \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma^{-1})
Target Dispersion (Sigma)  <---->  Prior Noise Dispersion (Sigma^-1)
[  Spread / High Variance  ]  --->  [ Hyper-Concentrated at Zero ]
[ Concentrated at Zero     ]  --->  [ Broad / Diffuse Tails       ]

3.3 Lipschitz Instability
When target \rho exhibits high directional variance, prior \mu_U becomes hyper-concentrated around zero [09:18].
* The transport map \nabla U(x) = \Sigma x must stretch concentrated samples across wide target domains.
* The Lipschitz constant \mathrm{Lip}(\nabla U) = \Vert{}\Sigma\Vert{}_{\mathrm{op}} explodes [09:46].
* This condition causes severe numerical instability, vanishing gradients, and failure on non-Gaussian manifolds [10:32].
4.0 SYSTEM MODIFICATION: CONJUGATE MOMENT MEASURES
To correct covariance inversion, the transport direction must be mathematically inverted [12:09].
4.1 Mathematical Formulation
Let \rho \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{ac}}(\mathbb{R}^d) be supported on a compact convex domain K \subset \mathbb{R}^d. There exists a lower semi-continuous convex potential W: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R} satisfying the Conjugate Moment Measure Factorization [11:20]:
\rho = (\nabla W^*)_\# \mu_W, \quad \text{where } d\mu_W(x) = \frac{1}{Z_W} \exp(-W(x)) \, dx
Applying convex duality identity (\nabla W^*)^{-1} = \nabla W yields the dual push-forward form:
\mu_W = (\nabla W)_\# \rho
Here, W is the Brenier potential that transports the target measure \rho directly to the base log-concave density \mu_W [12:42].
4.2 Covariance Rectification (Gaussian Benchmark)
For a Gaussian target \rho = \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma), the conjugate potential yields [15:30]:
\mu_W = \mathcal{N}\left(0, \Sigma^{1/3}\right)
* The covariance exponent shifts from negative -1 to positive +1/3.
* The prior dispersion scales monotonically with target variance.
* The generative transport map \nabla W^*(z) = \Sigma^{2/3} z possesses a bounded Lipschitz constant \mathrm{Lip}(\nabla W^*) = \Vert{}\Sigma^{2/3}\Vert{}_{\mathrm{op}}, preventing gradient explosion [16:06].
5.0 EXISTENCE PROOF VIA FIXED-POINT OPERATOR
The existence of W cannot be established via the classical variational arguments of Klartag or Santambrogio [14:33].
1. Operator Definition: Define the operator \mathcal{T} mapping convex potential V to the Brenier potential between target \rho and Gibbs density \mu_V: \mathcal{T}(V) = \mathrm{BrenierPotential}\left(\rho, \frac{1}{Z_V} e^{-V}\right)
2. Fixed-Point Equivalence: A potential W satisfies the conjugate moment measure factorization if and only if \mathcal{T}(W) = W [13:33].
3. Schauder Theorem Application: Because \rho has compact convex support, \mathcal{T} acts continuously on a compact convex subset of continuous functions in Banach space C(K). By the Schauder fixed-point theorem, \mathcal{T} possesses at least one fixed point W [14:33, 43:29].

6.1 Neural Network Parameterization
In dimension d > 1, closed-form quantile solutions do not exist [18:12, 20:52].
* Potential W_\theta is parameterized via an Input Convex Neural Network (ICNN) (Amos et al., 2017) [21:25].
* Layer weights satisfy non-negativity constraints W_{l} \ge 0, and activation functions are convex non-decreasing, ensuring x \mapsto W_\theta(x) is convex [22:00].
6.2 Dual Kantorovich Optimization
Train W_\theta by minimizing the relaxed Monge-Kantorovich dual objective [22:48]:
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mu_{W_\theta}}[W_\theta(z)] + \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \rho}[W_\theta^*(y)]
where W_\theta^*(y) = \sup_x \{\langle x, y \rangle - W_\theta(x)\}.
* Amortized Fenchel Conjugate: An auxiliary Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) predicts the optimal argument x^*(y) to warm-start gradient ascent on the concave sub-problem [24:14].
* Stochastic Single-Step Convergence: Full inner convergence of the Brenier map at each step is not required. Executing a single stochastic gradient step per mini-batch drives convergence of the fixed-point sequence [25:46].
6.3 Sampling via Langevin Monte Carlo
Because W_\theta is convex, base prior \mu_{W_\theta} \propto \exp(-W_\theta) is strictly log-concave. Draw samples z \sim \mu_{W_\theta} via Unadjusted Langevin Monte Carlo (LMC) [26:31]:
z_{k+1} = z_k - \gamma \nabla W_\theta(z_k) + \sqrt{2\gamma} \, \xi_k, \quad \xi_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_d)
The step size is scheduled adaptively against network curvature:
\gamma_t \approx \frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}(\nabla^2 W_\theta)}
where \lambda_{\max} is the spectral norm of the Hessian [27:46].
6.4 Push-Forward Generation
Push prior samples into the target domain using the conjugate gradient:
x = \nabla W_\theta^*(z) = \arg\max_{x'} \left\{ \langle x', z \rangle - W_\theta(x') \right\}
This step is evaluated rapidly via the amortized conjugate regression network [28:30].
7.0 MONGE-AMPÈRE DENSITY FORMULATION AND SECONDARY MODES
7.1 Exact Likelihood Evaluation
Applying the change-of-variables formula to \mu_W = (\nabla W)_\# \rho yields the Monge-Ampère partial differential equation at the density level [34:00]:
\log \rho(x) = -W(\nabla W(x)) - \log Z_W + \log \det\left( \nabla^2 W(x) \right)
This expression provides tractable, unnormalized log-likelihood access. It enables gradient-based image restoration, inpainting, and corrupt sample denoising by ascending \nabla_x \log \rho(x) [35:08].
7.2 Energy-Based Target Density Fitting
When empirical samples are unavailable but target density \rho(x) \propto \exp(-E(x)) is known up to a partition function:
* Regress the Monge-Ampère log-density directly against -E(x) [36:10].
* This optimizes W_\theta for unnormalized sampling without requiring standard MCMC chains in target data space [36:37].
8.0 SYSTEM LIMITATIONS AND OPEN DISCREPANCIES
1. ICNN Expressivity Bottleneck: Non-negative weight constraints limit the expressive capacity of ICNNs compared to standard UNet diffusion backbones [32:45].
2. Uniqueness Non-Identifiability: While the standard moment measure is unique up to translation [39:44], formal proof for the analytical uniqueness of conjugate potential W remains unestablished (though confirmed empirically) [37:43, 41:41].
3. Compact Support Restriction: The Schauder fixed-point theorem requires compactness of support K [43:29]. Relaxing this condition to arbitrary sub-Gaussian distributions remains an open theoretical objective [38:01].
